## 1. Executive summary

The `solve` concentrated-liquidity DEX (a lightly-modified **Orca Whirlpools** fork) is **substantially clean** — the swap, liquidity, token-transfer, tick/oracle, and math paths produced no confirmed vulnerabilities (only dead-code and no-op informational items). Risk is concentrated almost entirely in the **custom `vesolve` ve(3,3) program**, whose **reward / vault / voting subsystem contains multiple critical and high-severity defects**, and in the **`token`** program's initialization.

**Verdict: NOT deployment-ready.** In its current state the ve(3,3) reward system is **non-functional and unsafe**:

- The **entire reward and bribe payout mechanism cannot pay out at all** — every vault withdrawal reverts due to incorrect PDA signer seeds, so all deposited rewards/bribes would be **permanently locked** (`H-01`).
- Even once that is fixed, the **reward math systematically strands the majority of every vault** (`H-02`) and multiple **fund-safety holes** allow **free voting power** (`C-01`), **50× vote inflation** (`C-02`), **cross-token vault draining** (`H-05`), and **bribe theft by early voters** (`H-06`).
- The **`token` mint can be hijacked by front-running** its unauthenticated initialization (`H-07`).
- Transferring a lock **permanently bricks it** (`H-03`), freezing the principal for 50–100 years.

## 3. Detailed findings

### 🔴 C-01 — `increase_lock` lets an attacker mint unlimited voting power with no real deposit
**Status:** Confirmed (both verifiers: Critical) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** account substitution
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/increase_lock.rs:41-45,67-84`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/increase_lock.rs#L41)

**Description.** In `create_lock` the protocol vault destination is constrained `protocol_token_account.owner == protocol.key()` (`create_lock.rs:47`). In `increase_lock` the **same** account is constrained **only by mint** (`protocol_token_account.mint == token_mint.key()`, line 43) — the `owner == protocol` check is missing. `transfer_token_to_protocol` (`utils/token.rs:16-37`) transfers from `user_token_account` to `protocol_token_account` using `authority = user` (the signer), so the SPL CPI never requires the destination to be protocol-owned. An attacker can pass **any** correct-mint account as `protocol_token_account` — including their own account, or literally the same account as `user_token_account`, making the transfer a **net-zero self-transfer** — yet `lock.amount` and `protocol.total_locked` are credited by `amount` (lines 79-85) as if a real deposit occurred.

**Impact.** The invariant "`lock.amount` is backed by tokens in the protocol vault" is broken. `lock.amount` feeds voting power directly (`Lock::calculate_voting_power`; `vote.rs:77`), so the attacker gains **arbitrarily large voting power at zero cost**, capturing gauge weight / emissions and outsized reward claims. It also inflates the eventual unlock payout: `unlock_lock` pays `lock.amount` out of the **real** protocol vault, letting the attacker **withdraw more than they deposited and drain other users' locked principal**.

**Exploit.** (1) `create_lock` with `amount = 1`. (2) Momentarily hold `LARGE` tokens (flash-borrow works — the balance is untouched). (3) `increase_lock(id, LARGE)` passing `protocol_token_account = user_token_account` (only the mint is checked). (4) `transfer_checked(LARGE)` self-transfers (balance unchanged). (5) `lock.amount → 1+LARGE`, giving ~`LARGE` free voting power. (6) Vote to steer all emissions to the attacker's gauge; after maturity, unlock to drain the real vault.

**Recommendation.** Add `constraint = protocol_token_account.owner == protocol.key() @ ErrorCode::InvalidOwner` to `IncreaseLock` (mirroring `create_lock.rs:47`). **Better:** make the vault a fixed PDA (`seeds = [VAULT_SEED, …]`) and validate it by seeds in *every* instruction rather than by an owner-check on a caller-supplied account. Audit all instructions for the same missing constraint.

### 🔴 C-02 — `vote()` never caps total weight at 100%, allowing up to 50× voting-power allocation
**Status:** Confirmed (both verifiers: Critical) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** economic accounting
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/vote.rs:63-93`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/vote.rs#L63)

**Description.** `vote()` validates each *individual* gauge weight (`require!(vote.weight <= VOTE_WEIGHT_PRECISION)`, line 69 — each ≤ 100%) and accumulates `total_weight_percentage` (lines 64, 71-73), **but never checks that accumulator against 100%.** It is dead code (assigned, never read). A voter may therefore pass up to `MAX_GAUGE_VOTES` (50) gauges each at `weight = 100`. Each gauge then receives `current_voting_power * 100/100 = current_voting_power` (lines 82-93), so one lock allocates **up to 50× its real voting power**, spread across gauges. The invariant `sum(allocations) == voting_power` is broken.

**Impact.** Direct dilution/theft of emissions and bribes. An attacker assigning 100% to every funded gauge collects ~50× the per-gauge share of an honest voter who correctly splits weight, and inflates `epoch.total_voting_power` (diluting everyone else and stranding the remainder).

**Exploit.** Gauges `G1..G50` each funded with reward `R`. Attacker with power `P` votes 50 entries `{Gi, weight: 100}`. Each gauge records the attacker's full `P` (not `P/50`), so at claim time the attacker's weight in *every* gauge is `P`, capturing an outsized cut of all 50 vaults.

**Recommendation.** After the accumulation loop add `require!(total_weight_percentage <= VOTE_WEIGHT_PRECISION, ErrorCode::InvalidVotingPower)` (or `== 100` if full allocation is mandatory) **before** converting percentages to voting power.

### 🟠 H-01 — Vault withdrawals sign with incomplete PDA seeds → *all* rewards & bribes permanently locked
**Status:** Confirmed (independently by 4 finder agents; verifiers: High) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** CPI / PDA signer seeds
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/utils/vault.rs:41-52`](../programs/vesolve/src/utils/vault.rs#L41)
> **Note on severity:** rated High by consensus, but its *impact* is total, unrecoverable loss of availability of the entire reward/bribe subsystem — treat with Critical priority.

**Description.** Every vault PDA is derived with **three** seeds `[VAULT_SEED, current_epoch.to_le_bytes(), gauge.key()]` (`distribute_reward.rs:30`, `distribute_pool_reward.rs:30`), and `vault.bump` is the canonical bump for that full set. But `transfer_token_from_vault` — the **only** withdrawal path — signs the CPI with `signer_seeds = &[&[VAULT_SEED, &[vault.bump]]]` (line 41), **omitting the epoch and gauge seeds.** `create_program_address([b"vault", bump])` ≠ the real vault address that owns the vault ATA, so `invoke_signed` never grants the vault signer authority and the SPL `transfer_checked` fails the authority-is-signer check. (This helper was copied from the sibling `transfer_token_from_protocol`, which is correct only because the `Protocol` PDA is single-seed.)

**Impact.** `claim_reward` (`:137`), both `claim_pool_reward` transfers (`:165`, `:184`), and `claim_bribe_reward` (`:132`) **all revert**. There is no admin sweep/withdraw path either, so **every token deposited via `distribute_reward`, `distribute_pool_reward`, and `create_bribe` is permanently locked.**

**Recommendation.** Sign with the full seed set (the fields already live on `VaultData`):
```rust
let signer_seeds: &[&[&[u8]]] = &[&[
    VAULT_SEED,
    vault.epoch.to_le_bytes().as_ref(),
    vault.gauge.as_ref(),
    &[vault.bump],
]];
```
Add an **end-to-end integration test that actually completes a claim** — this class of bug is caught instantly by one real claim test.

### 🟠 H-02 — Reward-share formula divides per-gauge vaults by *global* voting power → most of every vault is stranded
**Status:** Confirmed (independently by 3 finder agents) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** reward accounting
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_reward.rs:107-127`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_reward.rs#L107) (same pattern in `claim_pool_reward.rs:131-161`, `claim_bribe_reward.rs:109-129`)

**Description.** All three claim paths compute
`claimable = rewards * pool_vote_share * user_vote_share / P / P`
where `pool_vote_share = total_gauge_vote * P / total_voting_power` and `user_vote_share = total_user_vote * P / total_gauge_vote`. This **algebraically reduces to `rewards * total_user_vote / total_voting_power`** — `total_gauge_vote` cancels. But `total_voting_power` is the **global** sum of *all* gauges' weights (`Epoch::update_total_power`, `epoch.rs:37-39`), while `rewards` is the **per-gauge** vault. So summed over a gauge's voters, only `rewards * (gauge_weight / total_voting_power)` is ever paid out; the fraction `1 − gauge_weight/total_voting_power` is **unclaimable**. There is no admin sweep/close/rollover, so the surplus is **permanently stranded**.

**Impact.** In any realistic multi-gauge deployment (the normal case), each gauge's global share is well below 1, so the **majority of every reward, pool-fee, and bribe vault is permanently locked** and voters are systematically underpaid. With `N` equally-voted gauges, each vault distributes only ~`1/N` of its funds.

**Exploit / illustration.** 10 gauges, voters split power so each gauge holds ~10% of total (`G/T = 0.1`). A 1,000,000-token bribe for gauge X → voters collectively claim only `1,000,000 × 0.1 = 100,000`; the other 900,000 are lost forever.

**Recommendation.** For a per-gauge vault, distribute purely by intra-gauge share: `claimable = rewards * total_user_vote / total_gauge_vote` (drop the `pool_vote_share` factor). If partial distribution is intended, add an admin/creator path to reclaim or roll over the remainder.

### 🟠 H-03 — `transfer_lock` permanently bricks the Lock (PDA seeded by owner, owner only changed in data)
**Status:** Confirmed (2 finder agents) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** PDA seed / owner binding
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/transfer_lock.rs:22-28,89`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/transfer_lock.rs#L22)

**Description.** The Lock PDA is derived from the current owner: `seeds = [VE_LOCK_SEED, user.key(), ve_token_id]` (used identically in `vote`, `increase_lock`, `extend_lock`, `merge_lock`, `split_lock`, `unlock_lock`, `transfer_lock`). `transfer_lock` moves the ve-NFT to the destination and sets `lock.owner = destination` (line 89) but **cannot relocate the account** — it stays at `PDA(original_owner, id)`. Every lock instruction simultaneously requires `seeds = [VE_LOCK_SEED, signer, id]` **and** `constraint = lock.owner == signer`. After a transfer these are **mutually unsatisfiable**: the new owner derives a different PDA (ConstraintSeeds failure); the old owner still derives the account but fails `lock.owner == user`.

**Impact.** After any transfer, **no one** can vote/extend/increase/merge/split/unlock the lock. Given locks are 50–100 years, the underlying principal held in the protocol vault is **effectively frozen forever**. The advertised transfer feature is non-functional and destroys value.

**Recommendation.** Do **not** seed the Lock PDA on the mutable owner. Key it on a stable identifier (e.g. `[VE_LOCK_SEED, ve_token_id]` or the ve-mint) and gate authorization via `lock.owner == signer` / ve-NFT possession (`amount == 1`). Apply consistently across all lock instructions.

### 🟠 H-04 — `vote()` accepts arbitrary gauge pubkeys (no Gauge-PDA / `is_active` validation)
**Status:** Confirmed · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** account substitution / DoS
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/vote.rs:67-123`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/vote.rs#L67)

**Description.** Each vote's gauge is taken purely from instruction data (`gauge_votes[].gauge: Pubkey`). `vote()` never loads a `Gauge` account, never checks the key is a Gauge PDA (`[GAUGE_SEED, protocol, pool_address]`), and never checks `gauge.is_active` (the field set in `create_gauge.rs:45` is **never read anywhere**). Arbitrary pubkeys are pushed into `epoch.gauge_weights` (lines 116-121) and summed into `epoch.total_voting_power` (line 126).

**Impact.**
1. **Reward dilution/stranding:** votes to non-existent gauges inflate `total_voting_power` (the denominator in the claim math), so honest voters on real gauges receive a smaller fraction and the phantom fraction is unclaimable → funds stranded.
2. **DoS:** `epoch.gauge_weights` is `max_len(100)` (`epoch.rs:13`). An attacker with two locks can fill all 100 slots with junk, after which any vote adding a 101st distinct gauge fails serialization — blocking new legitimate gauges from receiving votes that epoch.
3. Votes can target gauges intended to be inactive (`is_active` unenforced).

**Recommendation.** Pass the `Gauge` account(s) as validated accounts (or verify each pubkey via the `GAUGE_SEED` PDA) and require `gauge.is_active` before accepting a vote.

### 🟠 H-05 — `claim_reward` `token_mint` is unconstrained → cross-token drain of bribe/pool-reward vaults
**Status:** Confirmed (2 finder agents) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** missing mint constraint
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_reward.rs:28-44`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_reward.rs#L28)
> **Conditional:** currently masked by `H-01` (no vault withdrawal succeeds). Becomes **directly exploitable the moment `H-01` is fixed** — fix both together.

**Description.** `token_mint` in `claim_reward` has **no** constraint tying it to `protocol.token_mint` (contrast `distribute_reward.rs:37`), and the struct has no `protocol` account to bind against. `claim_reward` computes `claimable_fees` from `vault.rewards` (SOLVE-denominated) but then transfers that quantity of the **caller-chosen** `token_mint` out of `vault_token_account = ATA(vault, token_mint)`. Because the single vault PDA is the authority over the emission ATA **and** every bribe ATA **and** every pool-reward ATA (they share seeds), the SOLVE-denominated payout math can be applied against a different, more valuable token. The replay guard `user_rewards` is seeded **without** `token_mint` (`[USER_REWARDS_SEED, user, epoch, gauge]`), so it doesn't distinguish which token was drained; the user can *also* claim the real bribe via `claim_bribe_reward` (separate PDA).

**Impact.** A voter can withdraw their SOLVE-share *quantity* out of a **bribe or pool ATA** (e.g. take 100 USDC instead of 100 SOLVE), stealing funds earmarked for others and double-dipping.

**Recommendation.** Add `constraint = token_mint.key() == protocol.token_mint` (introduce `protocol` into the struct), and include `token_mint` in the `user_rewards` PDA seeds if multiple reward tokens are ever intended. Ideally use **distinct vault PDA namespaces** for emissions vs pool-rewards vs bribes so their ATAs can never be cross-addressed (see `P-01`).

### 🟠 H-06 — Bribe claim has no epoch-finalization gate → early voter drains the whole bribe
**Status:** Confirmed · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** economic accounting
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_bribe_reward.rs:55-107`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_bribe_reward.rs#L55)

**Description.** `claim_bribe_reward` computes payouts from the **live, still-mutable** epoch snapshot (`epoch.total_voting_power`, `gauge_weight.weight`, `user_gauge_vote.weight`). The only gating is `user_vote.epoch == epoch.epoch` and a matching vote/bribe entry — there is **no check that the epoch has ended** (no `epoch.end_time` comparison; no such error exists). Votes are still being cast (`vote()` only blocks the last 4h), and `create_bribe` has no time gate. Since the effective payout reduces to `rewards * user_weight / total_voting_power` (see `H-02`), whoever claims while `total_voting_power` is still small captures a disproportionate share; the **first/only voter** claims `rewards * (w/w) = the entire bribe`. The `user_rewards` PDA stops *them* re-claiming, but the vault ATA is already drained, so honest later voters' claims revert with `InsufficientFunds`.

**Impact.** Bribe funds stolen / mis-distributed via vote-finalization front-running.

**Recommendation.** Gate bribe (and reward) claims to **after** the epoch has ended — `require!(current_time >= epoch.end_time)` or a `finalized` flag set by `update_epoch` — so weights are frozen before any payout is computed.

### 🟠 H-07 — Unauthenticated `initialize_mint` → mint-authority hijack by front-running
**Status:** Confirmed · **Program:** token · **Category:** access control / initialization
**Location:** [`programs/token/src/instructions/initialize_mint.rs:21-73`](../programs/token/src/instructions/initialize_mint.rs#L21)

**Description.** `InitializeMint` only requires `authority` to be *a* `Signer` — no tie to the program upgrade authority, the `solve` hardcoded admins, or any allowlist. Line 72 unconditionally sets `mint_config.authority = authority.key()`. The mint PDA uses fixed seeds `[b"mint"]` with `init`, so it can be initialized exactly once — **the first caller after deployment becomes the permanent authority.**

**Impact.** The winner gains permanent, uncapped control: `is_minter` returns true for the authority (unlimited direct minting) and the authority solely controls `add_minter`/`remove_minter`. **Total compromise of the reward token.**

**Exploit.** On Solana, deployment and initialization are separate transactions. An attacker watching the chain submits `initialize_mint` with their own key before the deployer does; the deployer's later call reverts (account exists). The attacker now owns mint authority.

**Recommendation.** Gate `initialize_mint` on a hardcoded admin / expected upgrade-authority pubkey (require `signer == constant`, or constrain against the program's upgrade authority), or initialize **atomically** with deployment.

### 🟡 M-01 — `distribute_reward` and `distribute_pool_reward` share one vault and overwrite `vault.rewards`
**Status:** Confirmed · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** accounting state collision
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/distribute_pool_reward.rs:26-33,131-135`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/distribute_pool_reward.rs#L26)

**Description.** Both instructions `init_if_needed` the **same** `VaultData` PDA `[VAULT_SEED, epoch, gauge]`. `distribute_reward` sets `vault.rewards = amount` (emission tokens); `distribute_pool_reward` sets `vault.rewards = token_a_amount + token_b_amount` (pool tokens). Whichever runs **last** overwrites the other. `claim_reward` reads `vault.rewards` (`:85`) to size emission payouts, so the base becomes an unrelated figure in the wrong units.

**Impact.** In normal operation a gauge receives both emissions and pool fees each epoch, so emission payouts are computed from `token_a+token_b` — drastically over/under-paying and stranding emission tokens.

**Recommendation.** Give emission-reward and pool-reward vaults **distinct PDA seeds** and separate `VaultData` records, or store emission vs pool amounts in **separate fields**. (Overlaps with `H-05`/`P-01` — the clean fix is per-mechanism vault namespaces.)

### 🟡 M-02 — `claim_pool_reward` sizes payouts from the *live* vault balance → order-dependent underpayment
**Status:** Confirmed (2 finder agents) · **Program:** vesolve · **Category:** reward accounting
**Location:** [`programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_pool_reward.rs:107-108,143-161`](../programs/vesolve/src/instructions/claim_pool_reward.rs#L107)

**Description.** `rewards_a/b` are read from `vault_token_account_a/b.amount` (the **current** balance) and used as the pro-rata base. Every successful claim shrinks that balance, so each later claimer computes their share off a smaller number. (`claim_reward` avoids this by using the fixed `vault.rewards` — the two paths are inconsistent.)

**Impact.** Payouts are order-dependent: first claimer gets full share, later claimers progressively less; total distributed telescopes below the deposit and dust is stranded (no sweep path). Fairness/accounting bug, not a drain (share ≤ 1).

**Recommendation.** Snapshot the deposited pool amounts on `VaultData` at distribute time and compute each claim from that fixed snapshot (as `claim_reward` does).

### 🟡 M-03 — Hardcoded Helius RPC API key committed to source (frontend)
**Status:** Confirmed · **Repo:** frontend · **Category:** secret exposure
**Location:** [`src/providers/ClusterProvider.tsx:25-31`](../../frontend/src/providers/ClusterProvider.tsx#L25)

**Description.** The mainnet RPC endpoint is a committed literal with a live key: `https://mainnet.helius-rpc.com/?api-key=REDACTED` (line 27). ### 🔵 L-02 — No Content-Security-Policy / security headers (frontend)

## 6. Systemic observations & prioritized recommendations

1. **The `vesolve` reward/vault subsystem needs a redesign, not just point fixes.** `H-01`, `H-02`, `H-05`, `M-01`, `M-02`, and `P-01` are all facets of one architectural problem: **a single vault PDA and shared ATAs are reused across three reward mechanisms (emissions / pool fees / bribes) with inconsistent accounting bases and a copied-but-wrong signer.** Recommend: (a) separate PDA/ATA namespaces per mechanism; (b) a single consistent, snapshot-based pro-rata formula that divides a gauge vault by that gauge's own vote total; (c) explicit epoch finalization before any claim; (d) an admin sweep/rollover path for dust.
2. **Lock ownership model is broken for transfer.** `H-03` means the ve-NFT transfer feature cannot work as designed; the PDA-keying scheme must change across all lock instructions before any secondary-market/transfer functionality ships.
3. **Add real integration tests that execute the full happy path end-to-end** — create lock → vote → distribute → **claim** → unlock. The single most severe class here (`H-01`) reverts on the *first* successful claim and would be caught immediately.
4. **Harden initialization & authority.** Gate `initialize_mint` (`H-07`); confirm the on-chain program **upgrade authority** and the `solve` hardcoded `ADMINS` are the intended keys (ideally multisig) before mainnet.
5. **Update the on-chain `security.txt`.** `programs/solve/src/security.rs` still declares the Orca project (`name: "Orca Solve program"`, `orca.so`, `immunefi.com/bounty/orca`, `github.com/orca-so/solves`) — bug reports would be misrouted. Point it at solve3.fi. *(Informational; not counted above.)*
6. **Verify the lock-time design.** `MIN_LOCK_TIME = 50 years`, `MAX_LOCK_TIME = 100 years` (`constants/ve.rs`) is unusual for ve(3,3) — confirm this permanent-lock model is intentional, since it compounds the severity of any lock-freezing bug (e.g. `H-03`). *(Informational.)*
7. **Frontend:** rotate the leaked RPC key (`M-03`), lock down the image proxy (`L-01`), and add security headers/CSP (`L-02`) before production.

## 7. Methodology & limitations

**Approach.** 15 finder agents (Opus 4.8) each read an assigned component or applied a cross-cutting lens (account-validation, arithmetic, CPI/PDA) across all three programs plus the frontend, guided by a Solana/Anchor threat checklist (missing signer/owner/has_one, PDA substitution & seed collisions, reinit/rent theft, duplicate-mutable accounts, unchecked `remaining_accounts`, Token-2022 transfer-fee handling, overflow/`as`-truncation/rounding, reward-accounting & double-claim, epoch manipulation, oracle manipulation). Each raw finding was then re-verified by **two independent adversarial verifiers** that re-read the source — one testing **reachability** (can an attacker reach the path given Anchor constraints?), one testing **impact** (is there real loss/DoS, and is the severity right?). A finding is **Confirmed** only if *both* verifiers judged it real. Author manual review of the `vesolve` core was merged in. Total: **77 agents, 0 errors, ~1.6M tokens, 439 tool calls.**

**Limitations.** (1) Static review only — **no code was compiled, deployed, or dynamically fuzzed** (no Anchor/Solana toolchain in the environment; the audit was requested as review-only, no deploy). (2) No economic/game-theory simulation or formal verification. (3) The `solve` CLMM was reviewed for **deviations from stock Orca Whirlpools**, not re-audited in full — upstream-inherited logic is assumed to carry Orca's own audit history and was lower priority. (4) AI review can miss issues and can misjudge reachability; **confirmed ≠ exhaustive, and clean ≠ safe.** A professional human audit plus full integration/fuzz testing remains required before mainnet.
